============================
People
============================

Stakeholders
It might seem strange to include to the team someone traditionally thought of as being the client, and thus, outside of the team. It is indeed counter-intuitive given our everyday life experience. Yet, speaking out of my professional experience, I cannot stress more the importance of including the stakeholders in the developing team!
Ok, so who are the stakeholders? Simply put, they're people who have a vested interest in whatever we are building. Usually, the ones that pay the bill, but not necessarily if the product we are working on is of public interest. They can be our colleagues from the department next door, if we create a software solution or data science model for them.
Why is it so important to consider and include them in the team? Because they are the ones the know the subject matter. They also might be the end users for what we build. In both cases, their immediate feedback is of immense importance. Think for a minute that you have to write code for a financial institution, while you know nothing about the industry; definitely not good.
It is true that a dedicated business analyst could cover this need. If we believe it necessary, we can have a business analyst as part of the team. Still, the direct and immediate feedback from the stakeholders is invaluable and irreplaceable.

Product Owner
This is the person that has to deal with the question about what is the right thing to build. One of the usual problems is that teams build things that no-one uses. It is not trivial to figure out what is the right thing to build. There are different visions about what to build, and somehow, we have to decide. Then there is the interplay between the product/project direction and the technical aspects of it. Some features might be nice to have but difficult/costly to build with the technology or the given team skills and experience.
Therefore, we need someone that sits between the stakeholders and developers, knows everything about the product/project, keeps track of the questions asked and answers found about what to build. In more technical terms a product owner defines, prioritizes, and guides product development to deliver business and user value.

Developers
We use a generic term to denote all the people with technical skills that actually build the product. These might be software developers, dedicated software testers, data scientists, machine learning engineers, or whatever else is needed. 
As we have said before, the individuals that comprise an agile team tend to have certain characteristics that are linked to the way the team is supposed to work. For example, for the team to function properly we need team players rather than rockstars. We need team members with T-shaped skill profiles; that is a broad horizontal skill set along with depth for some of the skills. For example, that might be a developer with good test automation knowledge, or a data scientist that can follow ML-Ops practices. The objective is to make the team resilient and capable to tackle any technical difficulty that might arise.
